### Ground pork joins the livestock domain

A new species, `pig` (domestic pig), and a new product,
`ground_pork_1lb_package`, mirroring `ground_beef_patty`'s pattern: grinding
destroys the individual, so there is no anatomical floor and the floor is 1,
reachable only when a single hog is ground alone.

Per-hog trim yield (13.7-17.8 packages) comes from K-State Research and
Extension's Pork Cutout Table (a 205-lb hot carcass's "Trimmings ... Ground
pork and sausage" line, 7.8 percent) spread across a real hanging-carcass
weight range from WSU Extension. A loss chain (dressing, carcass-to-packaged)
carries a broader, separately-sourced yield figure from University of
Wisconsin-Madison Extension for narrative context, kept apart from the
product's own trim figure to avoid double counting.

The mixing cascade is honestly thinner than ground beef's: no DNA
mark-recapture study of pork grind-batch composition was found anywhere,
unlike beef's Hu et al. 2012. The likely reason is itself sourced - USDA
FSIS's 2015 grinding recordkeeping rule (9 CFR 320.1) requires source-lot
logs only for raw ground beef, never extended to pork, so the traceability
paper trail that produced the beef study never existed for this one. The
combo-bin pool (roughly 112-146 hogs) is therefore `project-estimate-mixing`
grade, reasoned from standard 2,000-lb combo bin capacity, and the cascade
stops at one stage rather than adding a second, unsourced grind-batch
multiplier the way ground beef's does.
